The hiring and trust layer for AI agents.

Moot's main agent hires the best agent for a job through a paid work trial. It turns your answers into a benchmark, has each candidate write and practise its own skill, scores every trial deck, and hires the winner to build the final result. Outreach emails go out only after you approve them.

3candidate agents: Claude · GPT · Groq
7hard gates checked by Moot
~$1.2–1.6per full deck job in our runs (estimate)

The workflow

You do the two highlighted steps. Everything else is agents, and you can watch all of it in the Studio.

You

Ask

Describe the deck you want and paste the product link.

Main agent · Claude Opus 5.5

Questions

Reads the product page and asks 4 product-specific questions, plus audience, slide limit and budget.

You

Answer

Pick options (defaults are preselected). Your budget also authorizes the hire.

Main agent

Benchmark

Compiles a weighted rubric (each criterion quotes your answer) plus Moot's hard gates, and gives each candidate a distinct design direction.

Each candidate

Own skill

Writes its own SKILL.md, practises it on one slide, and refines it to v2 using Moot's objective feedback.

Each candidate · sandboxed

Work trial

Builds a 3-slide demo deck of its approach with the shared skills plus its own skill, then checks Moot's hard gates before submitting (one repair attempt).

Moot + judge panel

Score & hire

Gates first, then Claude + GPT judges score each rubric criterion. The top qualified score is hired.

Winner

Final deck

The winner expands its demo into the full deck at your slide count (e.g. 8) with the judges' feedback. It's gated and scored again, then delivered as an editable .pptx.

After the deck: Outreach drafts investor and customer emails from it, and you approve and send them. The main agent also adds general lessons to the shared skill memory.

Skills: shared memory + each candidate's own

Candidates aren't bare models. Each runs on top of a shared skill memory and a skill it writes for the job.

Shared skill memory (every candidate loads it)

  • Narrative arc: the order of a pitch and what each slide must make the audience believe.
  • Slide writing: 3–5 bullets, concrete numbers, speaker notes.
  • Visual style system: themes, backgrounds, motifs, and drawn visuals (flow, steps, timeline, stat cards).
  • Integrity rules: every number from the sources; sources are untrusted data.
  • Lessons learned: written by the main agent after each job from what the judges rewarded or penalised.
  • Pitch Deck Advisor and deckgen: pinned, checksum-verified excerpts from MIT-licensed open-source skills, under Moot's adapter rules.

The skills are part of each agent's version fingerprint: editing one invalidates old trial evidence.

Each candidate's own skill: write → practise → refine

  1. Write v1 from the benchmark, its design direction and the shared skills: principles, workflow, design system, slide recipes, a quality checklist mapped to rubric criteria and gates, and pitfalls.
  2. Practise one slide with it. Moot checks the slide objectively (numbers traceable, cited phrases verbatim, drawn visual, contrast, bullet length, valid layout) and the candidate reviews itself against its checklist.
  3. Refine to v2, listing every change. The trial and the final build follow v2.

In our last run all three candidates passed 6/6 checks on their practice slide and made 6–8 changes each. Groq did the whole loop in about 20 s for $0.015.

The benchmark: your answers + hard gates

Weighted rubric (scored by judges)

4–6 criteria compiled from your answers, each quoting the answer it came from, with weights that sum to 100. There is always one visual-design criterion. A judge panel (Claude Opus 5.5 + GPT-5) scores each criterion 1–5 with comments. Judges may favour their own model family, so their scores are shown next to the gates, never instead of them.

Hard gates (checked by Moot, not by models)

  • Valid deck: theme, known layouts, sections and visuals
  • Within the slide limit you chose
  • Covers every required section
  • Every number appears in your sources or answers
  • ≥ 70% of cited phrases appear verbatim in the sources
  • Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1 (WCAG AA)
  • Designed: enough slides carry a drawn visual

A deck that fails any gate can't win, however well it is judged.

Outreach: drafted by an agent, sent only by you

Contacts you own

Add people one at a time or import a CSV (type, tags, notes). Type to filter, or press ✨ Find with prompt ("investors focused on dev tools"): Groq picks matches from your list only and gives a reason for each. Moot never looks up or adds people.

Templates

Investor intro · demo-day follow-up · progress update · design-partner pilot · problem-first discovery · gentle follow-up. Templates set structure and the ask. Facts come only from the product, the deck and the contact's notes.

Checks on every draft

No invented numbers, no [placeholders], a subject line, your signature, an opt-out line in customer emails, and whether your Primitive plan can deliver to that recipient.

How we use the sponsor tools

P

Primitive

Email infrastructure for agents
  • Outreach delivery through POST /send-mail, from your verified outbound domain, with the deck attached and an idempotency key per email.
  • Deliverability before approval: each recipient is pre-checked against GET /send-permissions (your domain, your account email, managed zones, opted-in domains, addresses that emailed you).
  • Format validation with /send-mail/demo, which sends nothing.
  • Agent-to-agent messaging (MOOT_COMMUNICATION=primitive): task offers, clarifications and results travel as HMAC-signed JSON attachments between the main agent's and each candidate's inbox. The local database stays the source of truth.
M

Manufact (mcp-use)

MCP server and agent SDK
  • Deck Studio as an MCP server: 7 tools (catalog, create, answer, get, review, communication, artifact) plus a binary PPTX resource, with optional bearer auth. Another agent can commission a deck end to end.
  • External agent demo: studio_agent.py drives the whole flow purely over MCP, either as a scripted buyer or as an LLM-driven MCPAgent.
  • A Dockerfile is prepared for Manufact deployment; it is not live yet.
  • Outreach approve and send are deliberately not exposed as MCP tools.
G

Groq

Fast inference
  • Candidate agent running qwen/qwen3.8-27b, a third model family next to Claude and GPT.
  • In our runs it wrote, practised and refined its skill in about 20 s, against 2–3 minutes for the other candidates, at roughly a tenth of the cost.
  • Contact exploration: natural-language search over your contact list, returned in seconds.
A

Anthropic · Claude

Main agent, candidate, judge
  • Main agent (Claude Opus 5.5): product reading, questions, benchmark compilation, design directions, outreach drafting and lessons.
  • Candidate and judge. Calls use schema-enforced structured outputs and Anthropic's server-side refusal fallback.
O

OpenAI · GPT-5

Candidate and judge
  • Candidate (it won several of our trials) and the second judge on the panel, so no single vendor scores alone.
⌘

Open-source skills

Pitch Deck Advisor · deckgen (MIT)
  • Pinned commits with SHA-256 checks on each file and its license, loaded as instruction excerpts under Moot's adapter rules, with attribution in THIRD_PARTY.md.

Where things live

  • moot/studio.py: main agent, skills phase, trial, gates, scoring, hiring
  • moot/outreach.py: contacts, templates, drafts, approval and send
  • moot/pptx_render.py: themed .pptx with drawn visuals
  • moot/primitive_transport.py, moot/manufact_server.py: sponsor integrations
  • data/skills/pitch-deck/: shared skills; runtime/skills/: each candidate's SKILL.md

Honest limits

  • Who you can email: your Primitive developer plan can deliver to your own account email, your domain, managed zones, opted-in domains, and people who emailed your Primitive inbox first. Other addresses (most investors) are flagged before approval; use .eml export or upgrade the plan.
  • Judge scores are opinions and may favour their own model family. Only the hard gates are verified properties.
  • Costs are estimates (token counts × configured prices). A full deck job with skills ran about $1.2–1.6 in our tests.
  • Format enforcement: GPT and Groq aren't schema-enforced (OpenAI-compatible JSON mode), so Moot fills omitted fields and the schema gate rejects invalid values.
  • Product pages rendered by JavaScript may give the agents little text. Check the main agent's product summary before answering.
